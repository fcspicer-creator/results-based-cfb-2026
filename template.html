How the rankings work

We rank what teams have earned on the field. No polls, preseason reputation, player evaluations, or betting odds.

Points from wins − penalties for losses + eligible unbeaten bonus
Wins build your résumé

Every win earns points. Beating a team with better results earns more. More wins help too.

Every loss costs points

Losing to a weaker opponent costs more. A close loss still costs points—there is no bonus for a “good loss.”

Unbeaten bonus: wins over FBS only

Any undefeated team—FBS or FCS—can earn this bonus. Only wins over FBS opponents increase it. Wins over FCS or non–Division I opponents add nothing to the bonus. Any loss removes it.

Small credit for how and where

Road wins earn a little extra. Larger wins help modestly, but the margin bonus stops growing at 28 points.

Opponents earn their strength

We use everyone’s results to judge opponents, repeating the calculation until the scores settle. No team starts with a ranking advantage.

Conferences earn their credit

Wins over other FBS conferences help, especially against stronger opponents. Last season’s postseason adds a small influence that fades each week.

See the exact formula and every modeling choice

Every FBS and FCS team starts with opponent quality 0.5; these are starting values for calculation, not preseason rankings. Results are recalculated recursively, with damping, until the maximum quality change is below 10⁻⁸. Quality = 0.5 + 0.28 × (score − FBS median) / FBS standard deviation (minimum spread 8), clipped to [0.04, 0.96]. The update blends 55% previous quality with 45% recalculated quality.

Win credit against any Division I opponent = 2 + 6 × adjusted opponent quality + margin bonus + 0.35 for a true road win. Margin bonus = (1 − exp(−min(margin, 28)/10)) / (1 − exp(−2.8)), capped at 1.

FCS strength in the combined ranking: first calculate the original FCS-only strength values. Then fit one common shift from completed FCS–FBS matchups, accounting for the participating teams’ original result-based strengths. Strong FCS teams stay stronger than weak FCS teams, but the subdivision’s overall results affect their shared scale. A strong FCS team can rate above a weak FBS team. Win credit depends on the opponent’s strength, with the same formula for FBS and FCS opponents.

Exact adjustment: let logit(q) = ln(q/(1−q)) and sigmoid(z) = 1/(1+exp(−z)). Fit shift d so Σ sigmoid(logit(qFCS) − logit(qFBS) + d) + 2 × sigmoid(d) = actual FCS wins + 1, summing across completed FCS–FBS games. The two smoothing games represent one win and one loss and prevent an infinite adjustment at zero/all wins. Adjusted FCS quality = sigmoid(logit(original FCS quality) + d). This fitted comparison is not a validated prediction probability. Game locations are not separately fitted in this subdivision adjustment; location remains a small contribution in game scoring.

Loss penalty = 2 + 6 × (1 − adjusted winner quality) + 0.30 for a true home loss. Every loss is negative, regardless of opponent. There is no close-loss credit. Neutral sites receive neither location adjustment.

Unbeaten bonus = 0.11 × (wins over FBS opponents)² for any team with no losses, otherwise zero. This applies equally to FBS and FCS teams in both views. Wins over FCS or non–Division I opponents do not increase the bonus; any loss removes it. Total score = win credits − loss penalties + unbeaten bonus. Unrounded scores determine order; exact ties use wins, then alphabetical team name.

Adjusted FBS opponent quality = 94% individual results quality + 6% conference context. Conference context uses total FBS interconference wins weighted by (0.5 + defeated opponent quality), normalized by the largest total. This rewards head-to-head victories and opponent quality. It rewards quantity, so larger conferences can benefit. Independents share an accounting group, not a claimed conference. No head-to-head override is applied: direct results already earn credit and penalties.

The 2025 postseason baseline sums interconference postseason victories weighted by 1 + the opponent’s full-season win percentage, normalized by the largest conference total. Same-conference postseason games count toward coverage but not conference strength. Baseline weight inside conference context = 0.20 × 0.80^(week − 1). Individual 2025 team records do not carry over as team ratings.

FCS schedules come from ESPN’s season-specific membership list and every FCS team’s regular-season schedule. FCS-only standings are calculated first using the existing full-credit Division I method and its internal FBS opponent-strength estimates. A subdivision adjustment is then fitted to cross-subdivision results. Those adjusted FCS strengths are held fixed while all teams’ combined résumé scores are recalculated. FCS-only evidence uses first-stage strength values; combined evidence and the FBS Top-25 use the adjusted calculation. FCS-only scores remain separate and are not mixed into the combined order. FCS opponents receive no FBS conference adjustment. Non–Division I opponent quality is 0; wins against them receive only 0.05 total points, no margin/site bonus, and do not increase the unbeaten bonus. All losses still count. The FBS Top-25 and Team evidence use combined scores; the FCS Top-25 uses FCS-only scores; FCS playoff games are excluded, and the existing December 7 update cutoff is unchanged. Source classifications include transitioning FBS programs. Weights are modeling choices, not empirically proven optimal values.

What we check—and what to keep in mind

Before publishing, we check that records and points add up, wins help, losses hurt, and game data pass our checks. If a check fails, the previous report stays online.

Some positions are close calls. We run eight extra calculations, changing one factor at a time by 20% in either direction. A team’s “rank range” shows its best and worst placement across those runs and the main calculation. It is a stability check, not a guarantee.

FCS opponents have individual results-based strength values. Both sides of each FCS–FCS game must appear and agree in the team schedules; FBS–FCS scores must match the FBS inputs. Canceled and postponed games are excluded until a final result is available. These cross-checks use ESPN data and do not independently guarantee completeness. Sparse FBS–FCS matchups limit how precisely the subdivisions can be compared. The two-stage calculation retains a separate FCS-only view and adjusts FCS strength for the combined comparison. Both calculations allow any undefeated team to earn a bonus based only on wins over FBS opponents. The adjustment is fitted to this season’s games, not proven optimal or independently validated; many cross-subdivision games occur at FBS venues. Combined résumé rank and opponent strength are different measures, and FCS-only scores cannot be directly compared with combined scores.

Full checks, source limitations, and model corrections

The build checks duplicate IDs, required fields, valid final scores, season and team coverage, future dates, missing past finals, record and point reconciliation, positive win credit, negative loss penalties, shuffled-input stability, and convergence. Only an allowlist of result and schedule fields reaches the model: polls, Elo, probabilities, rosters, and betting information are excluded.

The initial November 3 snapshot had all 624 included 2026 scores and 46 postseason scores checked against ESPN. This historical audit does not establish independent completeness of the schedule or verify all future games. Weekly updates reconcile recent ESPN scoreboards, which may not list every game. Rank ranges are sensitivity ranges, not statistical confidence intervals. Exact middle and bubble positions remain sensitive to the chosen weights.

Corrections from the recovered original model: neutral-site treatment and reversed home-loss flag fixed; individual FCS results replace the former flat 0.15 assumption; convergence required; unrounded scores determine order; same-conference postseason wins excluded from the baseline; deterministic tie resolution; weights exposed for sensitivity checks.
