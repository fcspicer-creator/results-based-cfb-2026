FCS Top-25

Each FCS team earns its own opponent strength from its results. This tab shows the separate FCS-only comparison. An undefeated FCS team earns a bonus only for its wins over FBS opponents. Team evidence also includes all FBS and FCS teams in one combined ranking, where FCS opponent strength is adjusted using results against FBS opponents. These are two labeled comparisons, not interchangeable scores.

FCS standings keep full game credit

For FCS standings, wins over FBS and FCS teams earn normal credit based on the opponent’s results. Every loss costs points. Only wins over FBS opponents increase an undefeated team’s bonus. FCS teams receive no FBS conference adjustment or 2025 conference baseline.

Non–Division I wins: +0.05

A win over a non–Division I team earns just 0.05 points, with no margin or road bonus. An undefeated FCS team earns a bonus only for its wins over FBS opponents. Losing to a non–Division I team still costs 8 points, plus 0.30 for a home loss.

How the rankings work

We rank what teams have earned on the field. No polls, preseason reputation, player evaluations, or betting odds.

Points from wins − penalties for losses + eligible unbeaten bonus
Wins build your résumé

Every win earns points. Beating a team with better results earns more. More wins help too.

Every loss costs points

Losing to a weaker opponent costs more. A close loss still costs points—there is no bonus for a “good loss.”

Unbeaten bonus: wins over FBS only

Any undefeated team—FBS or FCS—can earn this bonus. Only wins over FBS opponents increase it. Wins over FCS or non–Division I opponents add nothing to the bonus. Any loss removes it.

Small credit for how and where

Road wins earn a little extra. Larger wins help modestly, but the margin bonus stops growing at 28 points.

Opponents earn their strength

We use everyone’s results to judge opponents, repeating the calculation until the scores settle. No team starts with a ranking advantage.

A small FBS conference adjustment

FBS opponent strength gets a small adjustment from conference results, with a fading contribution from last season’s postseason. The separate Conferences tab compares all conferences using member résumés and wins over ranked opponents in our own combined list.

All conferences · résumé comparison

One list for FBS and FCS conferences, based on the results in this report. Half of the comparison score comes from the average combined résumé score of the conference’s teams. Half comes from net game points against other Division I conferences, including extra win credit for beating highly ranked teams, divided by conference size.

50% · Member-team résumés

Average the combined scores of every member. These scores already reward wins, penalize losses, account for opponent strength, and include the eligible unbeaten bonus. Strong and weak teams both count.

50% · Results against other conferences

Add win credit, give extra credit for beating highly ranked opponents, and subtract every loss penalty. Then divide by conference size. Opponent ranks come from our combined FBS/FCS Team evidence list, not an external poll.

One comparison score

Scale each component from 0 to 100 across the conferences in this report, then average the two. Higher is better. This is a comparison index, not a win probability. Independent groups are shown separately because they are not conferences.

Higher-ranked wins earn more: the rank bonus can add up to 100% of the original win credit. Beating No. 1 doubles that credit; beating a team halfway down the combined list adds about 25%; beating the last team adds no bonus. Credit rises smoothly toward the top. Loss penalties stay fully counted. Opponent ranks and conference scores recalculate with each Monday update, so a past win’s value changes as that opponent’s résumé changes.

Games within a conference are included in member-team résumés, but not in the outside-conference component. Non–Division I games remain in team résumés with the existing scoring rules; their records are shown in the head-to-head details but excluded from the Division I conference component. This comparison does not change team scores or replace the existing FBS conference adjustment.
