How the rankings work

We rank what teams have earned on the field. No polls, preseason reputation, player evaluations, or betting odds.

Points from wins − penalties for losses + unbeaten bonus
Wins build your résumé

Every win earns points. Beating a team with better results earns more. More wins help too.

Every loss costs points

Losing to a weaker opponent costs more. A close loss still costs points—there is no bonus for a “good loss.”

Stay unbeaten

Teams with no losses earn an extra bonus that grows with each win. One loss removes that bonus.

Small credit for how and where

Road wins earn a little extra. Larger wins help modestly, but the margin bonus stops growing at 28 points.

Opponents earn their strength

We use everyone’s results to judge opponents, repeating the calculation until the scores settle. No team starts with a ranking advantage.

Conferences earn their credit

Wins over other FBS conferences help, especially against stronger opponents. Last season’s postseason adds a small influence that fades each week.

See the exact formula and every modeling choice

Every FBS team starts with opponent quality 0.5. Results are recalculated recursively, with damping, until the maximum quality change is below 10⁻⁸. Quality = 0.5 + 0.28 × (score − FBS median) / FBS standard deviation (minimum spread 8), clipped to [0.04, 0.96]. The update blends 55% previous quality with 45% recalculated quality.

Win credit = 2 + 6 × adjusted opponent quality + margin bonus + 0.35 for a true road win. Margin bonus = (1 − exp(−min(margin, 28)/10)) / (1 − exp(−2.8)), capped at 1.

Loss penalty = 2 + 6 × (1 − adjusted winner quality) + 0.30 for a true home loss. Every loss is negative, regardless of opponent. There is no close-loss credit. Neutral sites receive neither location adjustment.

Undefeated bonus = 0.11 × wins², otherwise zero. Total score = win credits − loss penalties + undefeated bonus. Unrounded scores determine order; exact ties use wins, then alphabetical team name.

Adjusted opponent quality = 94% individual results quality + 6% conference context. Conference context uses total FBS interconference wins weighted by (0.5 + defeated opponent quality), normalized by the largest total. This rewards head-to-head victories and opponent quality. It rewards quantity, so larger conferences can benefit. Independents share an accounting group, not a claimed conference. No head-to-head override is applied: direct results already earn credit and penalties.

The 2025 postseason baseline sums interconference postseason victories weighted by 1 + the opponent’s full-season win percentage, normalized by the largest conference total. Same-conference postseason games count toward coverage but not conference strength. Baseline weight inside conference context = 0.20 × 0.80^(week − 1). Individual 2025 team records do not carry over as team ratings.

Complete FCS schedules are absent from the 2026 source. All FCS opponents receive a common quality of 0.15, an explicit assumption that does not distinguish strong FCS opponents. Source classifications include transitioning FBS programs. Weights are modeling choices, not empirically proven optimal values.

How conferences compare

Conferences earn credit by beating teams from other FBS conferences. Beating a stronger opponent earns more credit. The first number adds up that credit; the second scales it from 0 to 1 and includes the fading 2025 postseason influence.

These are totals, so larger conferences can benefit. Independent teams share a bookkeeping group, not an actual conference.

What is conference context?

A small adjustment for the strength of an opponent’s conference. It comes from that conference’s wins over other FBS conferences, with more credit for beating stronger teams. It makes up 6% of an opponent’s adjusted strength; the other 94% comes from that team’s own results.

What does “blended” mean?

It means we mix two ingredients: this season’s conference results and a small, fading contribution from the 2025 postseason. Both are scaled against the leading conference in their respective periods before we combine them.

How do I read the number?

The blended context is on a 0–1 scale: higher means stronger conference results in this model. A value of 0.75 is a strength score, not a 75% win rate or a 75% chance of winning. It is already included in game points—not added again.

What we check—and what to keep in mind

Before publishing, we check that records and points add up, wins help, losses hurt, and game data pass our checks. If a check fails, the previous report stays online.

Some positions are close calls. We run eight extra calculations, changing one factor at a time by 20% in either direction. A team’s “rank range” shows its best and worst placement across those runs and the main calculation. It is a stability check, not a guarantee.

All FCS opponents receive the same strength value because we do not have their full schedules. We verify included scores, but cannot independently guarantee that the source lists every game.

Full checks, source limitations, and model corrections

The build checks duplicate IDs, required fields, valid final scores, season and team coverage, future dates, missing past finals, record and point reconciliation, positive win credit, negative loss penalties, shuffled-input stability, and convergence. Only an allowlist of result and schedule fields reaches the model: polls, Elo, probabilities, rosters, and betting information are excluded.

The initial November 3 snapshot had all 624 included 2026 scores and 46 postseason scores checked against ESPN. This historical audit does not establish independent completeness of the schedule or verify all future games. Weekly updates reconcile recent ESPN scoreboards, which may not list every game. Rank ranges are sensitivity ranges, not statistical confidence intervals. Exact middle and bubble positions remain sensitive to the chosen weights.

Corrections from the recovered original model: neutral-site treatment and reversed home-loss flag fixed; FCS assumption explicit; convergence required; unrounded scores determine order; same-conference postseason wins excluded from the baseline; deterministic tie resolution; weights exposed for sensitivity checks.
